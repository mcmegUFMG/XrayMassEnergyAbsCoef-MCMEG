              Scientific Overview
            
            

              Extending Mass Energy-Absorption Coefficients Down to 0.05 keV
            


            
            

              Notice: This database is currently under development. Simulated mass attenuation coefficient data for selected materials in the sub-keV energy range will be made available following publication of the associated scientific research.
            


            

              In microdosimetry, low-energy X-ray brachytherapy, radiation therapy, and nanoparticle-enhanced radiotherapy, a substantial fraction of energy deposition arises from ultrasoft photons and fluorescence cascades in the sub-kiloelectronvolt regime (0.05 keV to 1.0 keV), below the 1 keV lower limit of standard reference tabulations. This database, developed by Lucas F. Araújo↗, Celso G. Paulo↗, and Telma C. F. Fonseca↗ — members of the Monte Carlo Modelling Expert Group (MCMEG)↗ at UFMG — provides dedicated μen/ρ evaluations obtained through Monte Carlo code simulations across the 0.05 keV to 1.0 keV energy range for:
            

            
    
              
  • 
                Elemental Media: Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn, Pd, Ag, I, Gd, Hf, Ta, W, Pt, Au, and Bi (modeled as 100 wt% pure elements).
              
    • 
              
  • 
                ICRU 44 Tissues and Compounds: Liquid Water, Adipose Tissue, Whole Blood, Cortical Bone, Brain (Grey/White Matter), Breast Tissue, Eye Lens, Lung Tissue, Skeletal Muscle, Ovary, Testis, and Four-Component Soft Tissue.
              
    • 
            

          


          
          

            

              Low-Energy Dataset (0.05 keV to 1.0 keV): Values in the 0.05 keV to 1.0 keV energy range were generated and compiled by the Monte Carlo Modelling Expert Group (MCMEG) to provide extended low-energy mass energy-absorption coefficient (μen/ρ) data for research and educational applications.
            

            

              High-Energy Reference Dataset (1 keV to 20 MeV): Reference values for energies from 1 keV to 20 MeV are reproduced for comparison and continuity purposes from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Standard Reference Database 126:
            

            

              Hubbell, J.H. and Seltzer, S.M. (2004), Tables of X-Ray Mass Attenuation Coefficients and Mass Energy-Absorption Coefficients (version 1.4). [Online] Available: https://physics.nist.gov/xaamdi [Accessed: November 2026]. National Institute of Standards and Technology, Gaithersburg, MD.
